# Tropical QFT

This repository accompanies the [mini-course on tropical quantum field theory](https://events.perimeterinstitute.ca/category/131/) at Perimeter Institute, 2–5 November 2026.

> Tropical geometry is a promising new tool in quantum field theory. It helps in two ways. First, it gives faster methods that make hard QFT calculations doable, including some that were long out of reach. Second, it offers fresh insight into deep questions about QFT, such as why and how its perturbative expansions tend to diverge. This mini course is a gentle introduction into tropical quantum field theory methods and possible applications.

This repository collects background material on tropical QFT, e.g. the relevant research literature.

## Papers

More papers will be added. Suggestions for relevant papers are welcome via pull request or comment.

- *Tropicalized quantum field theory and global tropical sampling* (M. Borinsky),
  [arXiv:2508.14263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14263)  
  Introduces tropical QFT and global tropical sampling.
  These are the basis of the course.

- *Renormalized tropical field theory* (P.-H. Balduf, E. Panzer),
  [arXiv:2512.21091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21091)  
  Renormalized tropical φ⁴ theory.
  Compares tropical and non-tropical quantities explicitly and shows that they behave similarly.

- *Tropical QFT and the sine-Gordon model* (M. Borinsky, J. Radkovski, S. Rajalingam Kannagi),
  [arXiv:2610.12653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12653)  
  Tropical QFT in D=2, fully renormalized. In this case the theory can be described very explicitly.
  The RG fixed points of tropical QFT are studied in detail.

- *Tropical Monte Carlo quadrature for Feynman integrals* (M. Borinsky),
  [arXiv:2008.12310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12310)  
  Introduces tropical sampling. Individual Feynman integrals can be evaluated much faster than with earlier methods.
  The underlying structure is that of generalized permutahedra.

- *Hepp's bound for Feynman graphs and matroids* (E. Panzer),
  [arXiv:1908.09820](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.09820)  
  Introduces the Hepp bound, which one could also call the tropical Feynman integral.
  Proves many of its relevant properties.

- *Tropical sampling from Feynman measures* (M. Borinsky, M. Fraaije),
  [arXiv:2504.09613](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09613)  
  Sampling from measures distributed like Feynman integrals.
  Tropical sampling makes this more efficient.

- *Bayesian integrals on toric varieties* (M. Borinsky, A.-L. Sattelberger, B. Sturmfels, S. Telen),
  [arXiv:2204.06414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06414)  
  Applies tropical sampling to problems from algebraic statistics.
  Generalizes the method to toric varieties.

- *Tropical Feynman integration in the Minkowski regime* (M. Borinsky, H. J. Munch, F. Tellander),
  [arXiv:2302.08955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08955)  
  Generalizes tropical sampling to Feynman integrals with Minkowski external kinematics.
